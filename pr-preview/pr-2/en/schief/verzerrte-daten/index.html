The machine says about Krümel: cat.

The shelter knows what Krümel really is: dog.

With JavaScript you can change the shelter's bag here and test new animals. The bag holds Luna, Mimi, Flocke and Minka (cats, all small) and Bruno and Rex (dogs, both big). Krümel (dog: pointed ears, short snout, small, round pupils) has 3 features in common with Luna and with Mimi, and 2 with Rex: the machine says “cat”. Otto (dog: floppy ears, short snout, small, round pupils) has 3 in common with Flocke: “cat” again. If you put Otto into the bag, Luna, Mimi and Otto each have 3 features in common with Krümel: the machine does not know.

Exercise

Krümel and Otto are both small dogs. Why does the machine say “cat” for both?

  • In the bag, all the small animals are cats. right answer

    There are no small dogs in the bag. So a small cat is most similar.

  • The program counts the features wrongly.

    The numbers in the table are right.

  • Small dogs nearly always look like cats.

    Both have round pupils like all the dogs on our cards.

That all the small animals in the bag are cats is a chance result of the choice. The machine takes it on as a pattern.

Bias: the fault is in the data

The program is fine. But the bag shows the world in a one-sided way: cats only small, dogs only big. The machine finds a pattern in it that real animals do not have, and so it makes the same mistake again and again: small dogs with a short snout become cats.

bias

The training data shows part of the world in a one-sided way. The learning system takes on a pattern that only holds in the data and keeps making the same mistakes.

Remember

If a learning system keeps making the same mistake, look at the training data first. Which examples are missing?

In real systems too

In 2018, Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru studied three programs that work out a person's gender from a photo. Two widely used collections of face photos were 79.6 % and 86.2 % made up of people with lighter skin. The error rate in working out the gender was up to 34.7 % for women with darker skin, and at most 0.8 % for men with lighter skin. (Gender Shades, 2018)

In which order do you find and fix a fault in the training data?

Give each entry its place: 1 for the first.

Right answer: 1. Collect the examples that were recognised wrongly, 2. Look for what these examples have in common, 3. Check whether examples like them are missing from the training data, 4. Add the missing examples and test again

Only the pattern of the mistakes shows which examples are missing.

Looking back

The choice of training data decides what a learning system can do. If examples are missing, it takes on chance features of the choice as patterns and keeps getting things wrong in the same way. What goes into the bag decides what comes out.
